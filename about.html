01 / About

The long
game.

A career built on persistence, curiosity, and a refusal to take the easy path.

Data science is a subject that found me, rather than the other way around. I was asking so many questions in class, it was insane how evidently I enjoyed what I was learning. I have not stopped since.

Where it started

My first real exposure to data was a Diploma in Business Information Systems at Republic Polytechnic in Singapore. It was my introduction to coding, databases, SQL, business intelligence, and analytics. Tears were cried. But there was a satisfaction in arriving at the point of comprehension after much difficulty and solving something independently that I could not ignore.

My managing director at the time advised me against pursuing a Bachelor's degree in BIS, so I pivoted to Arts. A lot of it felt like a confusing detour, but the Statistics for Social Sciences course stopped me in my tracks. It was cognitively laborious in a way that produced an addicting feeling of achievement at the end. That feeling has become something I keep chasing.

The years in between

After graduating, I continued pursuing tech in any capacity I could. I joined W.Media, a data centre, cloud and cyber security events and publishing company. I got exposure to founders, award winners, and sponsoring global companies. It was exciting. But I was hired as a Marketing Executive and quickly grew depressed. I knew this was not where I was meant to be.

For nearly nine years, I worked in food and beverage alongside other roles, saving every pound I could toward postgraduate education. It was not glamorous. But it was deliberate.

The MSc

In 2023, I enrolled in the MSc Data Science in Business programme at Regent's University London. From the very first weeks, it was clear this was the right place. I graduated in 2024 with distinction, with SAS Academy accreditation, and with an invitation to contribute to an academic textbook on data science applications.

The subject did not feel like study. It felt like recognition.

Why precision medicine

I want to find my Ikigai: work that does not just pay the bills, but contributes something real to the world. Precision medicine is a high-level goal that I may never fully reach in my lifetime. But if I shoot for the moon, I might land amongst the stars.

The promise of precision medicine is treatments designed for the individual, not the average patient. The idea that data science could help get the right therapy to the right person at the right time is the most compelling application of this field I can imagine. That is the long game I am playing.

I am analytical by nature, resourceful by experience, and I have been told I have grit. I hope that is evident in what I continue to build.

What the long way round taught me

A non-traditional path teaches you things a direct one does not. Slogging at minimum wage jobs for nearly a decade to save for education makes you value the destination far more than someone who arrived easily. It builds resourcefulness, resilience, and a genuine empathy for people who are working hard in systems that were not built for them.

I have particular empathy for women and minorities in this male-dominated industry. It shapes the kind of colleague I want to be: one who welcomes knowledge-sharing, supports others, and does not take any opportunity for granted.
